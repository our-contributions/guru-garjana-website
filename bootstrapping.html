Dhol: building your foundation

Three sounds make every dhol rhythm. Directions below are for right-handed players.

Ta

The thapi side, the left head. Soft, melodic tones.

Dhin

The thoka side, the right head. Deeper, resonant tones.

Dha

Both heads struck together. A full, powerful sound.

Zanj: light, rhythm and impact

The zanj (cymbals) bring crispness, control and choreography to every Dhol-Tasha rhythm. Mastering them is about movement, grace and precision, not just sound.

Posture is key

Slightly bent knees keep you grounded for sharp, quick moves.

Led by your feet

Most beats follow deliberate steps: alternating, pivoting or shifting weight.

Motion plus sound

You don't just play the zanj. You embody the rhythm.

Taal 1Basic grounded strikeWatch →Move: bend both knees slightly to ground yourself. Beat: one clean, powerful strike on 1.
Taal 2Power in threeWatch →Move: a soft knee dip on each count of three. Beat: 1 – 2 – 3, one beat per dip.
Taal 3Flow and alternationWatch →Move: alternate feet, right, left, right, left. Beat: 1 – 2 – 1 – 2, even and steady.
Taal 4Rooted strikeWatch →Move: step the right foot forward, leaning your weight into it. Beat: one strong strike as the foot lands.
Taal 5Three-step forward energyWatch →Move: right foot forward with a triple-count bend. Beat: 1 – 2 – 3, one beat per step.
Taal 6Push and pullWatch →Move: right foot forward, then back in a controlled step. Beat: 1 on the forward step, 2 on the return.
Taal 7Center control crossoverWatch →Move: right foot to center, then left foot to center, crossing gently. Beat: a strike on each center tap, 1 – 2.
Transition beatForward driveWatch →Move: step forward for 4 counts, one step per beat. Beat: 1 – 2 – 3 – 4. Great for changing formation or closing a phrase.
Taal 8 (optional)Full circle energyNo video yetMove: right foot twice on the right side, left foot forward, then pivot 180° to face the other way. Beat: 1 – 1 – 2 – pivot, with a pause after the pivot.

Quick answers

Do I need an instrument to start?

No. Practice the dhol on your lap and the tasha with any sticks until you're ready to buy your own.

When do I buy my instrument?

Only after your teacher approves, so you know you're fully prepared.

Is there ongoing support?

Yes. Mentors are always around in the WhatsApp group.

Do the lessons cost anything?

No. Our lessons are free. No tuition, just passion.
